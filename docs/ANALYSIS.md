# Financial method and limits

The original Python analysis is preserved in `engine/analysis_rules.py` and adapted by `engine/tickertwister.py`; `engine/engine.py` normalizes Yahoo data for the desktop UI. The original 39 sections include base and trend rules. Effective score = earned available weighted points / available weighted maximum × 100. Missing criteria are excluded, and coverage is a count rather than weighted coverage. User weighting scales a criterion’s contribution without changing its underlying point fraction.

The migration uses explicit yfinance methods and normalized statement aliases, handles missing/invalid denominators, and exposes actual annual dates rather than implying that seven years are always available. The historical scoring model uses at most four annual statements. Additional comparison history is limited to the observations actually returned; five years is a maximum, not guaranteed availability. Original-source notices remain intact.

Current/TTM values, annual histories, long-window averages and user assumptions are distinct. Read the period labels and formulas. Financial firms and unusual accounting structures may need sector-specific models. A high score does not prove a durable competitive advantage or predict returns.

AI-AIC receives the active saved company score/weights, criteria, ratios and history, with a visible input preview. User valuation assumptions and share-price targets are excluded from its quality-focused assessment. The conclusion is a research triage, not a personalized recommendation or trade instruction. Audit the statements and business risks independently.

## Owner Earnings DCF

The model selects the cash-flow calculation from the company's reported sector and industry. American Express is treated as a deposit-taking bank holding company, even though its Yahoo industry is Credit Services. For Berkshire Hathaway, the insurance model is an approximation for a group with additional operating businesses. The app applies the same cash-flow definition to each historical year and the starting estimate. [American Express 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/4962/000000496226000080/axp-20251231.htm) · [Berkshire Hathaway 2025 annual report, note 26](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/2025ar.pdf)

| Business activity | Annual Owner Earnings estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Banks and insurers | Net income serves as a proxy for cash flow to equity. Regulatory capital requirements may reduce distributable earnings. |
| Other financial services, including payment networks | Net income + depreciation and amortization − total capital expenditure. The model excludes working-capital movements, including settlement balances. |
| Other businesses | Net income + depreciation and amortization − estimated maintenance capital expenditure + the signed working-capital cash effect reported on the cash-flow statement. Maintenance spending is estimated at the lower of depreciation and total capital expenditure. |

If an adjustment required by the selected formula is missing, the model uses zero in that calculation and identifies the assumption by fiscal year. The reported field stays empty. Net income remains essential for the annual estimate. For example, the VEEV fiscal year ending January 2026 yielded USD 854.940 million from USD 908.906 million net income, USD 37.972 million depreciation and amortization, zero assumed maintenance capex and USD 91.938 million of working-capital outflow.

**Starting value.** When the three most recent consecutive fiscal years have estimates, compare the latest value with their arithmetic mean. If the absolute difference exceeds 30% of the absolute mean, the default uses the mean. At exactly 30% it uses the latest year. With incomplete history it uses the latest available fiscal year's estimate, if calculable. Inputs remain editable. The source breakdown retains the latest year's figures and shows the three-year average separately.

**Growth.** Calculate uncapped compound annual growth rates for adjusted Owner Earnings, net income and revenue from positive endpoints. The elapsed years are the actual days between fiscal dates divided by 365.25. For financial companies use the historical revenue CAGR when available, then net income growth, then adjusted Owner Earnings growth. If all three are unavailable the growth field stays empty. Turnover growth provides an observable starting assumption even when margins, interest income or settlement flows fluctuate. The analyst can change the projection. For operating companies, average available distinct signals with equal weights using the same fiscal endpoints. When adjusted Owner Earnings growth is more than ten percentage points above both income and revenue growth, or below both by that amount, use net income growth. Each resulting scenario has a −20% floor. The two upper limits are 10% and 12%. The primary DCF starts with the 10% limit and an editable 2% perpetual growth assumption. A manual forecast can also be entered when the history is insufficient.

**Discounting.** Project ten annual flows by multiplying the starting value by (1 + annual growth) to the power of the projection year. Discount each by (1 + discount rate) to the same power. Terminal value at year ten equals year eleven's cash flow divided by (discount rate − perpetual growth). Discount that terminal value by ten years. For banks and insurers, the sum of the present values is equity value directly. For other companies, the current model deducts net debt once from the sum and divides the remaining equity value by the outstanding share count. Net income already includes financing costs, so this additional deduction can double count financing effects.

For banks and insurers the model discounts at the cost of equity in the cash-flow currency. A user can supply a missing beta or enter a personal cost of equity. The dated CAPM estimate remains available as a reference. For payment networks and other non-bank financial services, the standard DCF starts with the available cash-flow-currency WACC, subject to the model floor. For an operating company, or when that WACC is unavailable, the model starts with the greater of 6% and a recent ten-year government yield in the cash-flow currency. If the observation is unavailable the floor is 6%. A higher personal discount rate is available. Price comparisons require a compatible share and currency basis. Margin of safety equals 1 − price / model value, for a positive model value. Upside equals model value / price − 1, for a positive price.

Earlier dossiers keep their original saved data. On opening an earlier valuation, a starting value or growth assumption matching the earlier automatic model is recalculated for the working copy. Other personal starting values, growth forecasts and terminal growth assumptions carry forward. The sector and currency rules apply to the working copy and its scenarios.

### Cost of capital

For banks and insurers the initial equity discount rate is Re = Rf + β × 5%. A user-entered cost of equity changes the discounting assumption and leaves the CAPM reference visible. Debt and deposits underpin the business, so the model values equity directly. Sources include the dated risk-free rate, Yahoo beta (or an explicitly supplied beta) and the assumed five-percentage-point market risk premium.

For other companies WACC = (E / V) × Re + (D / V) × Rd × (1 − T), with V = E + D. E is market capitalization and D is gross debt including reported lease obligations. The weights require comparable units. Corporate borrowing costs inferred from reporting-currency financials cannot be converted into a different borrowing rate by a spot FX transaction.

Interest expense, income tax expense and pretax income use a trailing twelve-month window ending on the balance sheet date. A completed fiscal year ending on that date covers the same window. With positive pretax income the effective rate is tax expense divided by pretax income, bounded between 0% and 35%. A loss, missing information or zero pretax income triggers the disclosed country reference. An unknown country uses a disclosed 25% modeling assumption. For a debt-free balance sheet, Rd = 0 and WACC = Re. For positive debt, divide absolute TTM interest expense by debt and clip the result to [Rf, Rf + 10 percentage points]. If matched interest expense is missing, use Rf + 1.5 percentage points before applying those bounds.

The reference series depends on currency. USD uses the ten-year US Treasury benchmark, EUR the German ten-year Bund, GBP the ten-year UK gilt, CHF the Swiss Confederation ten-year bond, JPY the Japanese government ten-year par yield, and CAD the Canadian government ten-year benchmark. The observation and source are displayed with the calculation. An unsupported currency or a missing, future-dated or stale observation leaves the benchmark unavailable. Legacy US yields stored with EUR dossiers are excluded. The fixed 5% market risk premium is a common modeling assumption. Refreshing the financial data also retrieves the applicable benchmark.

### Worked example

For starting Owner Earnings of €200 million, annual growth of 9%, a discount rate of 6%, perpetual growth of 2.5%, net debt of €115.4 million and 812,838 shares, the projected cash flows have a present value of €2,339.31 million. The terminal value is €13,865.99 million at year ten, or €7,742.69 million today. The sum is €10,082.01 million. Subtracting net debt leaves €9,966.61 million, or €12,261.49 per share. The figures describe a dated Lotus Bakeries scenario. Fresh Yahoo data, a different benchmark observation or a different maintenance estimate will change the result.
